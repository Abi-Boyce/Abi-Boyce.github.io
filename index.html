Rules vs. AI ticket triage for an education platform

A live prototype that sorts educator support tickets with simple rules first, sends only the unclear ones to Claude, and keeps people in charge of student-safety cases.

OpenClose

The problem: support teams at fast-growing AI companies get a flood of tickets, and not every one needs an AI model to sort it. What I built: a working triage board for 30 teacher support tickets. Keyword rules handle the clear-cut ones, Claude handles the ambiguous ones, and the page scores rules, Claude and the combined approach against my own labels. The rule: use AI where it earns its place, and send anything about student privacy or safety to a person. The tickets are fictional.

What it shows

  • When a deterministic rule beats a model: billing, password and roster tickets.
  • Where AI helps: tickets whose meaning sits in tone or context.
  • How to measure it: category accuracy, priority accuracy and catching every urgent ticket.

How it would run in production

  • A Salesforce flow applies the rules to new cases.
  • Unmatched cases call Claude through n8n or Zapier and write back a category, priority and confidence.
  • Low-confidence and safety cases go to a human queue, with a weekly re-score against a labeled set.

Try it: open the live prototype, copy the prompt into Claude, and paste the reply back in to see the scores update.

Approval vs. loss: a lending analysis in SQL and Python

39,747 real LendingClub loans, explored in SQL and modeled in Python to see who to approve and what it costs in losses.

OpenClose

The question: who should a lender approve, and how much does a better risk signal reduce losses? What I built: DuckDB SQL to clean and explore public LendingClub data, and a Python default model trained on 2007–2010 loans and tested on 2011. At an 80% approval rate it cut the bad rate from 11.9% to 11.1% compared with grade alone. I also generated adverse action reasons, left out location to avoid proxies for protected traits, and found that over half of declines came down to loan term, which points to offering a shorter term instead of a decline. How I worked: built with an AI assistant, with every result re-run and spot-checked.

See it: open the analysis and the SQL and Python code.

  • Drop in enrollment questions to HR

AI-powered outreach pipeline (built in Zapier)

A live automation that turns a saved contact into a personal LinkedIn note that points to the right portfolio project, with a person reviewing every message before it’s sent.

OpenClose

The problem: personal outreach works, but writing a thoughtful note for every person takes time, and generic templates get ignored. What I built: a four-step Zap that drafts a short, specific message for each new contact and matches it to the most relevant project on this site. The rule: the AI drafts and I decide. Nothing sends automatically.

The live Zap

How it works

StepToolWhat it doesWhy it matters
1. TriggerApolloStarts when I save a new contact: name, title, company, LinkedIn.One click is the only manual step before review.
2. MatchFormatter (lookup table)Maps the company to the portfolio project built for that kind of customer, with this homepage as the fallback.Every message links to proof that is relevant to the reader.
3. DraftAI by ZapierWrites a warm connection note under 300 characters, using the contact’s first name, title and company, plus the matched link. Output is a structured text field so later steps can use it reliably.Personal at scale, within LinkedIn’s character limit.
4. QueueZapier TablesSaves the contact, matched project and draft to an “Outreach Drafts” table with status Draft.A human review queue, so nothing goes out unchecked.

What I had to solve

  • Apollo’s free plan doesn’t include a CRM connection, so I connected it through an API key instead.
  • The AI step failed until its output field was set as a structured Text type, which I fixed and retested.
  • Google Sheets wasn’t a reliable target, so I switched to Zapier Tables.

Guardrails

  • Drafts only. I review, edit and send every message myself.
  • Each step was tested with sample data before going live.
  • Real contacts’ details stay private and are never published here.

Where this came from: when I started appointment setting, I wasn’t happy with the scripts I was given, so I used AI to help me rewrite them. My bookings went from 3–5 a week to 10–15. This pipeline uses the same approach for outreach: AI handles the first draft, and my judgment decides what gets sent.

CS reporting & automation pipeline (Python + SQL)

A working pipeline that turns CRM, support and usage data into health scores, a renewal watchlist, a ticket triage queue and AI-ready business review inputs, with data-quality checks built in.

OpenClose

The problem: CS teams often build reports by hand, one request at a time, from data spread across the CRM, the support desk and the product. What I built: a small reporting layer that does it automatically: SQL models the data, Python runs the checks and rules, and an LLM drafts business review notes from a minimized summary. The rule: numbers come from SQL, words can come from AI, and a person approves anything a customer sees. All data is fictional. See the code on GitHub.

How it works

StepBuilt withWhat it doesWhy it matters
1. LoadPython + DuckDBReads CRM accounts and renewals, support tickets and weekly product usage.One place to query, instead of three exports.
2. CheckSQL checksFlags missing renewal dates, duplicate accounts, tickets tied to unknown accounts and missing usage weeks.Problems get fixed at the source before anyone trusts the numbers.
3. ModelSQL mart viewsBuilds clean account, adoption and support tables, then a health score with every component in its own column.CSMs can see why a score moved, not just that it did.
4. ActSQL + rulesLists renewals due within 120 days that aren't healthy, each with a next action. Routes open tickets to queues by rule.Turns a report into a to-do list.
5. DraftLLM prompt + human reviewWrites QBR brief drafts from a small JSON summary per at-risk account, plus triage suggestions for tickets the rules can't place.AI saves the writing time; a person checks every number.

  • Only aggregates go to the LLM: no user names, emails or raw ticket text.
