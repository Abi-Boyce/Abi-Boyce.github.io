90-day AI adoption plan

Taking a new customer from kickoff to everyday use, with a clear measure of success at each stage.

OpenClose

Customer: a fictional 250-person healthcare operations team rolling out an AI writing and summarizing tool. Goal: 70% of licensed users active every week by day 90.

Days 1–30

Launch

  • Kickoff with the sponsor: agree on goals and how success is measured.
  • Interview 5–8 users to find their most repetitive tasks.
  • Pick 3 starter use cases, such as summarizing patient calls.
  • Run the first training session for a pilot group.

Success: pilot group active weekly; 3 use cases live.

Days 31–60

Expand

  • Recruit and train the first champions.
  • Roll out to more teams with role-based training.
  • Weekly office hours for questions.
  • Collect quick wins and share them company-wide.

Success: 50% weekly active; 1 champion per team.

Days 61–90

Prove value

  • Survey users on time saved and confidence.
  • Find teams that are lagging and give them focused support.
  • Value review with the sponsor: results, stories, next goals.
  • Plan the next set of use cases.

Success: 70% weekly active; value review delivered.

What I’d track

  • Weekly active users
  • Use cases in daily use
  • Time saved, reported by users
  • User confidence before and after training

Early warning signs

  • A team with no usage after training
  • Sponsor misses two check-ins
  • Users say they don’t trust the output

Training workshop: “AI at Work, Your First 60 Minutes”

A hands-on session for non-technical teams who are new to AI.

OpenClose

Audience: 15–25 people with little or no AI experience. By the end, everyone will: complete one real task with AI, know how to check its answer, and know where to get help.

0–5 min
WelcomeAsk what tasks eat up their week. Their answers shape the session.
5–15 min
What AI is good and bad atPlain-language examples, including a confident wrong answer.
15–30 min
Live demoThree tasks from their own work, step by step.
30–45 min
Hands-on practiceEveryone tries one task, with me and a champion walking the room.
45–55 min
Checking the answerA simple 3-question check: Is it accurate? Is it supported? Is it safe to send?
55–60 min
Next stepsA one-page cheat sheet, office hours and an invitation to become a champion.

Why it works

  • Starts from their real tasks, not features.
  • Everyone leaves with one success.
  • Builds trust by showing mistakes, not hiding them.

Follow-up

  • Day 2: email with recording and cheat sheet.
  • Week 2: check usage and reach out to anyone not using it yet.
  • Week 4: short survey on confidence and time saved.

Champion program plan

Finding and supporting the power users who spread adoption from the inside.

OpenClose

Why champions: people learn new tools fastest from a coworker they trust. Target: one champion for every 15–20 users.

1. Recruit

Look for early adopters in training and in usage data, plus managers’ suggestions. Invite them personally.

2. Equip

A champion kit: tips, a demo script, answers to common questions and early access to new features.

3. Connect

A monthly champion call and a shared chat channel to swap wins and flag problems early.

4. Recognize

Share their wins with leadership and thank them publicly, so being a champion feels worth it.

What champions do

  • Help teammates with first tasks
  • Share one useful tip a week
  • Tell me what’s confusing or not working

How I’d measure it

  • Usage on teams with a champion vs. without
  • Questions answered by champions
  • New use cases champions find
