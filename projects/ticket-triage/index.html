How the hybrid decides


    

  • Rules go first. If exactly one category's keywords match, the rule wins. It is instant, free, and easy to audit.
    • 

  • Claude takes the rest. Tickets with no match or a tie go to Claude with the category definitions and must return JSON with a confidence score.
    • 

  • People keep the risky ones. Anything Claude labels Privacy & student safety, or scores below 0.7 confidence, goes to a human queue, no matter what.
    • 



When AI is the wrong tool


Billing, password resets and roster syncs have clear words and clear owners, so a rule or a Salesforce flow handles them better than a model. AI earns its cost on tickets where the meaning sits in tone or context, like a parent seeing another family's report or a story that isn't safe for 7-year-olds.






Taking it to production


    

  1. A record-triggered Salesforce flow on new Cases applies the keyword rules and sets Category, Priority and a "Routed by" field.
    2. 

  2. Unmatched Cases call Claude through an n8n or Zapier step (or Agentforce), which writes back Category, Priority, Confidence and a one-line reason.
    3. 

  3. Low-confidence and safety Cases go to a human review queue in Salesforce.
    4. 

  4. A 50-ticket labeled set is re-scored every week. If accuracy drops, the prompt or rules get updated before anyone notices the misroutes.
    5. 



What I'd watch


  • P1 recall: safety and outage tickets must never sit in a normal queue.
  • Misroute rate and re-assignments per week.
  • Time to first touch, before and after.
  • Share of tickets the rules handle, which is the cost lever.


Limits: 30 synthetic tickets is a small sample, and I wrote both the tickets and the keywords, so the rules score is likely higher than it would be on real tickets. Testing on real ticket history would be the next step.
